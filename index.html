The Monetary Sector & The South African Reserve Bank

Core Theory
🪙 Functions & Evolution of Money
Money overcomes the barter economy's double coincidence of wants.
  • Medium of Exchange: Generally accepted in payment for goods and settlement of debt.
  • Unit of Account: Common denominator quoting relative prices.
  • Store of Value: Retains purchasing power across time (eroded by inflation).
South African currency is fiduciary money 💡: intrinsically worthless paper backed by public trust and legal tender law.
📊 Monetary Aggregates (M1, M2, M3)
M1 = Currency outside banks + Demand deposits
M2 = M1 + Quasi-Money (Short & Medium-term deposits)
M3 = M2 + Long-term deposits (Comprehensive Measure)
SARB defines M3 as the broadest and most reliable monetary indicator.
🏦 Commercial Bank Credit Creation
Commercial banks create money by advancing credit through fractional reserves.
Credit Multiplier 💡: m = \frac{1}{r}
Where r is the cash reserve requirement ratio (e.g., if \(r = 2.5\%\), \(m = 40\)). Constrained by statutory reserve ratios, capital adequacy, and credit demand.
📈 Liquidity Preference 💡 & Liquidity Trap 💡
Total money demand \(M_d = L_1(Y) + L_2(i)\):
  • Active Balances \(L_1(Y)\): Transactions & precautionary demand; positive function of income \(Y\), insensitive to interest rates.
  • Speculative Balances \(L_2(i)\): Idle cash; inverse function of nominal interest rate \(i\) (downward-sloping).
Visual Derivation: Money Market Equilibrium & Policy Transmission
SARB Repo Transmission: Repo Rate 💡 (\(\uparrow\)) ➔ Prime Rate (\(\uparrow\)) ➔ Credit Cost (\(\uparrow\)) ➔ Domestic Expenditure \(C, I\) (\(\downarrow\)) ➔ AD (\(\downarrow\)) ➔ Demand-pull Inflation cools (\(\downarrow\))
Study Unit 2 • Chapter 15

The Public Sector & Fiscal Policy

Core Theory
⚖️ Market Failure & Public Goods 💡
Pure Public Goods: Non-rival in consumption and non-excludable in access (e.g. National Defense, lighthouse).
  • Free-Rider Problem 💡: Non-excludability allows individuals to enjoy benefits without paying, causing private underproduction and requiring state tax financing.
  • Common Property Resources: Rival and non-excludable (leads to the tragedy of the commons).
🏭 Negative Externalities 💡
Pollution generates external costs unpriced by competitive markets.
MSC = MPC + Marginal External Cost (MEC)
Free market produces where \(P = MPC\), causing overproduction (\(Q_1 > Q_2\)). Solved via a Pigouvian Tax 💡 (\(t = \text{MEC}\)) to internalize costs and eradicate Deadweight Loss 💡.
🧾 Tax Classifications & Equity
  • Progressive Tax 💡: Average Tax Rate (ATR) rises as income rises (e.g. South African PIT, 18%–45%).
  • Proportional Tax: ATR remains constant across all income tiers.
  • Regressive Tax: ATR falls as income rises. VAT is regressive relative to income because lower-income households consume a larger share of disposable income.
  • Fiscal Drag 💡: Inflation pushes nominal incomes into higher brackets without real purchasing growth.
🏛️ National Budget & Fiscal Stances
Budget Deficit: G > T \implies \text{Financed via Treasury Bonds}
  • Expansionary Fiscal: \(G \uparrow\) or \(T \downarrow\) (boosts AD during economic downturns).
  • Contractionary Fiscal: \(G \downarrow\) or \(T \uparrow\) (dampens demand-pull inflation).
  • Crowding Out: Extensive state borrowing raises interest rates, suppressing private capital investment.

Study Unit 3 • Chapter 20

Inflation & Price Instability

Core Theory
🔥 4 Pillars of Inflation Definition
Inflation is NOT an isolated price increase. It strictly requires:
  1. A dynamic, continuous process over time.
  2. A considerable, significant percentage increase.
  3. Affecting the general price level across the economy.
  4. Erosion of the real purchasing power of money.
📏 Measurement: CPI 💡 vs. GDP Deflator 💡
\pi = \frac{CPI_t - CPI_{t-1}}{CPI_{t-1}} \times 100
\text{GDP Deflator} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}} \times 100
Scope Contrast: CPI includes imported consumer goods (petrol, electronics) but excludes capital goods. GDP Deflator includes all domestically produced final goods and services but strictly excludes imports.
🌪️ Demand-Pull 💡 vs. Cost-Push 💡
  • Demand-Pull: AD shifts right. \(P \uparrow\), Real GDP \(Y \uparrow\), Unemployment \(\downarrow\).
  • Cost-Push (Stagflation 💡): AS shifts left (oil shock, Rand depreciation, wage spikes). \(P \uparrow\), Output \(Y \downarrow\), Unemployment \(\uparrow\).
SARB targets headline CPI inflation within the 3% to 6% band.
💸 Redistribution Consequences
  • Borrowers (Debtors): Benefit because debt is repaid with devalued currency.
  • Lenders (Creditors) & Pensioners: Suffer because fixed nominal returns buy fewer goods.
  • Exporters: Lose competitiveness if domestic inflation exceeds trading partner inflation.
Visual Derivation: Cost-Push Stagflation in the AD-AS Framework
The Stagflation Dilemma: Cost-push shifts \(SAS\) left, forcing price levels up (\(P_0 \rightarrow P_1\)) while output contracts below potential (\(Y_f \rightarrow Y_1\)), creating a severe unemployment gap.
