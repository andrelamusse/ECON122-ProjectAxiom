Study Unit 1 • Chapter 14

The Monetary Sector & The South African Reserve Bank

Core Theory
🪙 Functions & Evolution of Money
Money overcomes the barter economy's double coincidence of wants.
  • Medium of Exchange: Generally accepted in payment for goods and settlement of debt.
  • Unit of Account: Common denominator quoting relative prices.
  • Store of Value: Retains purchasing power across time (eroded by inflation).
South African currency is fiduciary (fiat) money: intrinsically worthless paper backed by public trust and legal tender law.
📊 Monetary Aggregates (M1, M2, M3)
M1 = Currency outside banks + Demand deposits
M2 = M1 + Quasi-Money (Short & Medium-term deposits)
M3 = M2 + Long-term deposits (Comprehensive Measure)
SARB defines M3 as the broadest and most reliable monetary indicator.
🏦 Commercial Bank Credit Creation
Commercial banks create money by advancing credit (loans) through fractional reserves.
Credit Multiplier: m = \frac{1}{r}
Where r is the cash reserve requirement ratio (e.g., if \(r = 2.5\%\), \(m = 40\)). Constrained by reserve ratios, capital adequacy, and borrower creditworthiness.
📈 Liquidity Preference Framework
Total money demand \(M_d = L_1(Y) + L_2(i)\):
  • Active Balances \(L_1(Y)\): Transactions & precautionary demand; positive function of income \(Y\), insensitive to interest rates.
  • Speculative Balances \(L_2(i)\): Idle cash; inverse function of nominal interest rate \(i\) (downward-sloping).
🎯 The SARB Accommodation System & Transmission Mechanism
The SARB does not set money supply exogenously. It forces commercial banks into a daily liquidity deficit and accommodates this shortage at the Repo Rate via repurchase auctions.
Repo Rate (\(\uparrow\)) ➔ Prime Lending Rate (\(\uparrow\)) ➔ Cost of Credit (\(\uparrow\)) ➔ Spending \(C, I\) (\(\downarrow\)) ➔ Aggregate Demand (\(\downarrow\)) ➔ Inflation Cools (\(\downarrow\))

The Public Sector & Fiscal Policy

Core Theory
⚖️ Market Failure & Public Goods
Pure Public Goods: Non-rival in consumption and non-excludable in access (e.g. National Defense, streetlights).
  • Free-Rider Problem: Non-excludability allows individuals to enjoy benefits without paying, causing market failure and requiring tax funding.
  • Common Property: Rival and non-excludable (leads to tragedy of the commons / overexploitation).
🏭 Negative Production Externalities
Pollution generates external costs unpriced by competitive markets.
MSC = MPC + Marginal External Cost
Market equilibrium overproduces (\(Q_1 > Q_2\)) at too low a private price (\(P_1 < P_2\)). Corrected via Pigouvian Taxes (\(t = \text{MEC}\)) to eliminate Deadweight Loss (DWL).
🧾 Tax Classifications & Equity
  • Progressive Tax: Average Tax Rate (ATR) rises as income rises (e.g. South African PIT, 18%–45%).
  • Proportional Tax: ATR remains constant across all income levels.
  • Regressive Tax: ATR falls as income rises. VAT is regressive relative to income because poor households spend a larger fraction of income on taxable goods.
🏛️ National Budget & Fiscal Stances
Budget Deficit: G > T \implies \text{Financed via Treasury Bonds}
  • Expansionary Fiscal: \(G \uparrow\) or \(T \downarrow\) (shifts AD right to combat recessions).
  • Contractionary Fiscal: \(G \downarrow\) or \(T \uparrow\) (dampens demand-pull inflation).
  • Crowding Out: Massive government borrowing pushes interest rates up, discouraging private investment.
Study Unit 3 • Chapter 20

Inflation & Price Instability

Core Theory
🔥 4 Pillars of Inflation Definition
Inflation is NOT a once-off price increase. It strictly requires:
  1. A dynamic, continuous process over time.
  2. A considerable, significant percentage increase.
  3. Affecting the general price level across the economy.
  4. Erosion of the real purchasing power of money.
📏 Measurement: CPI vs. GDP Deflator
\pi = \frac{CPI_t - CPI_{t-1}}{CPI_{t-1}} \times 100
\text{GDP Deflator} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}} \times 100
Scope Contrast: CPI includes imported consumption goods (crude oil, laptops) but excludes capital goods. GDP Deflator includes all domestic production but excludes imports.
🌪️ Demand-Pull vs. Cost-Push Inflation
  • Demand-Pull: AD shifts right. \(P \uparrow\), Real GDP \(Y \uparrow\), Unemployment \(\downarrow\).
  • Cost-Push (Stagflation): AS shifts left (wages, oil shock, Rand depreciation). \(P \uparrow\), Output \(Y \downarrow\), Unemployment \(\uparrow\).
SARB targets headline CPI inflation within the 3% to 6% band.
💸 Economic Redistribution Consequences
  • Borrowers (Debtors): Benefit because they repay loans in depreciated currency.
  • Lenders (Creditors) & Pensioners: Harmed as fixed nominal returns lose purchasing power.
  • Fiscal Drag: Nominal wage gains push taxpayers into higher brackets without real income growth.
